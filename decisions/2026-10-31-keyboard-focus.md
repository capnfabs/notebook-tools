# Keyboard focus: what takes it, and how the transcript uses it

Tab, Shift-Tab and the arrow keys move the keyboard focus; page commands are on ⌘←/→ (see `2026-10-31-arrow-keys-move-focus.md`). Commands with text-editing keys are off only while a text field has the focus (`text_edit_focused`), not while any widget does. A focused corner handle keeps the arrows to nudge itself (1 pt, or 10 pt with Shift); the nudges count as one move once the keys are let go, so the page is cleaned once. Thumbnails and the uncertain words' boxes on the page aren't in the Tab order. ⌘←/→ already go through the pages, and the keyboard corrects a word in the text. Reason: Human Taste (thumbnails and word boxes out of the Tab order); agent recommendation accepted for the corner nudge.

In the transcript, focus works like the pointer: the focused word and its line are highlighted, and focus scrolls into view. Enter on a marked word opens its popup with the focus on the first choice. Enter on the rest of a line edits it. Escape, a pick or the end of a line edit puts the focus back on the piece it came from, if it's still there. If it isn't, such as deleted struck text, focus goes by place in the line and lands on what follows. Once the focus is in the popup, Tab and Shift-Tab go round its choices and don't leave it, so Escape or a choice is the way out (Human Taste). A popup opened with the mouse doesn't take the focus. Changing page while the transcript has the focus puts it at the top of the new page's transcript (Human Taste). Placing all four corners by keyboard is out of scope. Reason: agent recommendation accepted.
