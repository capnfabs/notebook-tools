# HEIC through ImageIO on macOS, libheif elsewhere

On macOS, HEIC decodes through ImageIO (`CGImageSource`), so the shipped binary links nothing outside the OS. Homebrew's libheif pulls in GPL x265 and aom (encoders we never use), its builds are arm64-only and tied to the build machine's macOS, and every bundled dylib would need its install name rewritten and re-signing. ImageIO also brings Apple's licensed HEVC decoder (no patent question) and works on iOS. Other platforms keep the libheif `Decoder` behind `cfg(not(target_os = "macos"))`. They'll ship libheif + libde265 as separate dylibs without x265: the app needn't be open source, and LGPL is fine dynamically linked but not statically, so not `embedded-libheif`. JPEG and PNG stay on the `image` crate everywhere.

ImageIO hands over unrotated pixels plus an EXIF-style orientation it reads from the HEIC's `irot`/`imir` boxes, even with the EXIF stripped. It's applied with the same `apply_orientation` as JPEG. Pixels keep their own colour space (Display P3 from iPhones), with no conversion to sRGB, as libheif and the `image` crate do. Cached transcriptions are kept, since Apple's decoder differs from libde265 only imperceptibly. Reason: Human Taste (ImageIO on mac, libheif without x265 elsewhere, no open-source requirement, keeping the cfg'd libheif code, raw colour, keeping the cache); unquestioned agent recommendation for orientation via `apply_orientation` and ImageIO for HEIC only.
